I developed the HUD, menus and gamepad input, and connected combat to the maintenance cycle. My work also included supply rules, aim assist and infantry hit reactions. Teammates built fortress locomotion and the core enemy and recovery systems.

HUD and minimap

The player needs to watch their own health, protect the fortress and keep track of several weapons. I grouped the health gauges and route around the minimap, with the equipped weapon and ammunition on the other side of the screen.

Select a screenshot to view it at full size. These are captures from development.

Health and navigation

Player and fortress health share a circular display. The map rotates with the view and distinguishes enemies, allies and the fortress. A fortress outside the map range stays marked at its edge, while the route distinguishes traveled and remaining sections.

Weapon and ammunition display

Switching weapons updates the portrait and highlights the active slot. The loaded magazine and reserve magazines are shown separately. Weapon and reload state drive the material parameters used by the ammunition display.

Keeping the display in step with gameplay

Gameplay objects own health and ammunition. The C++ widget reads those values and passes display parameters to UMG materials. Ammunition text and material parameters are rewritten when the relevant values change. Damaged health leaves a short trail before draining to the new value.

Which weapon receives an ammunition pickup

A pickup first supplies the equipped finite-ammo weapon if it has room. Otherwise, it chooses the weapon with the fewest reserve rounds relative to magazine size. I preserved capacity limits and prevented duplicate collection. A valid pickup is consumed even when health or ammunition is already full.

Combat, maintenance and the next departure

A day/night cycle tied to combat

I connected daytime combat, sunset, night maintenance and sunrise to the encounter state. Combat waits for surviving enemies to be defeated before ending. Maintenance can be finished early through player input, and the sun, moon and exposure change with each phase.

From the opening to the result screen

I extended the existing redeployment flow for an automatic initial drop, holding fortress departure until the player had recovered control. I also connected the opening and victory/defeat sequences to the menus, with input restored at the end of the sequence.

Menus, gamepad input and language

I connected the title, pause, settings and result screens to game state. The Widget Blueprints keep the layout editable; C++ handles transitions, focus, settings and input mode.

Returning control to the right place

Opening a menu switches to UI-only input and focuses its initial button. Hovering a button also updates focus. Closing settings returns focus to the menu; resuming restores the appropriate gameplay input mode. This keeps navigation and firing from sharing the same input accidentally.

Updating prompts when the device or language changes

A local-player subsystem observes keyboard, mouse and gamepad input. When the active device changes, the HUD and menus refresh their prompts using the current bindings. English and Korean text use a String Table, and language changes notify existing widgets so dynamic prompts update too. The selected language is saved for the next launch.

Input during cinematics

I connected cinematic input capture and a hold-to-skip prompt to the menu flow. Gameplay keys held before the transition are cleared, and control is restored after the sequence. Gamepad landing selection also uses the stick to move the target instead of relying on a mouse ray.

Aim assist that respects stick input

I added reduced sensitivity near a target and a limit on corrective rotation. A neutral stick does not turn the camera. Steering away from the target disables rotation correction, so the assist does not keep pulling against the player’s input.

Weapon hit reactions and explosion knockback

Hit reactions had to fit the existing damage, death and recovery flow. I added a temporary physical response to the hit body region and short weapon-specific staggers, while leaving the living character’s capsule in control of movement.

A hit body that recovers to animation

The hit resolves to a valid physics body. If a capsule hit does not identify a usable body, a safe fallback is selected. Only that branch is simulated, the physics weight fades out, and the previous collision and animation-tick settings are restored. Death and corpse collection end the temporary reaction before continuing their own flow.

Canceling the attack already in flight

Stopping an animation alone would leave a scheduled melee impact able to fire. On stagger, I clear the impact timer and pending attack flags, stop the montage and interrupt movement. The escort controller also respects stagger while following and attacking.

Tuning the launch distance

Explosion launch velocity falls off with distance from the impact. Living infantry use a character launch, while a dead body receives the velocity through its physics body. After feedback that enemies flew too far, I reduced horizontal and upward speeds and checked the near/far relationship again. Damage is passed separately from the knockback calculation.

Distance-based launch calculation

RecycleWeaponComponent.cpp

    const float InnerRadius = FMath::Clamp(ExplosionInnerRadius, 0.f, Radius - 1.f);
    const float Falloff = 1.f - FMath::Clamp((Distance - InnerRadius) / (Radius - InnerRadius), 0.f, 1.f);
    FVector Direction = Offset.GetSafeNormal2D();
    if (Direction.IsNearlyZero()) Direction = FVector::ForwardVector;
    return Direction * FMath::Max(0.f, ExplosionLaunchSpeed) * Falloff
        + FVector::UpVector * FMath::Max(0.f, ExplosionUpSpeed) * Falloff;

Grenade-launcher rotation and sound

Each successful shot advances the launcher drum by one chamber, with the motion controlled by a Curve. When several weapon representations are visible, only one plays the rotation sound. Switching weapons or ending play stops the rotation and cleans up its audio.

Integration and release

I integrated changes from my Diversion branch and resolved source conflicts. While connecting combat feedback and UI to the team’s enemy, recovery and fortress code, I corrected heavy-enemy approach and recovery behavior, and conflicts between recall and gamepad input. I also tuned wave and maintenance defaults.

The team published a Windows build and gameplay video on itch.io for the 2026 Epic AI MegaJam. The work shown here covers my gameplay, combat feedback and interface contributions.

Development checks
